# Comp gap

> MOCK DATA. Written for the Caddy Lab prototype to stand in for a real recruiting best-practice corpus.

## What's really going on

When a candidate says the number is low, nine times out of ten they haven't done the maths on the whole package yet, or they're anchoring to a figure someone else gave them. The worst move is jumping straight to the client to ask for more. You lose credibility with both sides and you still don't know what the real gap is. Your job is to find out three things: the number they actually need, the number they'd love, and where that number came from. Those are rarely the same figure. A candidate who says 'I need 180' often means 'my mate at a bigger company is on 180 and I'd feel silly taking less'. That's an ego gap, not a mortgage gap, and you handle it completely differently.

## Dig deeper

1. When you say it's lower than you hoped, what number did you have in your head, and where did that number come from?
2. If the base landed exactly where you want, what else about this role would still give you pause?
3. What's the number where you'd stop thinking about money and start thinking about the work?
4. If I went back to them with that, and they got close but not all the way, what would make the difference for you?

## Watch for

Candidates who won't name a number are usually using another offer as the anchor. Pivot to the competing process ladder.
